Can an icy moon constrain the parameters required for planets/moons to host life?

A research project exploring Enceladus, a Saturnian moon whose hidden ocean and orbital history offer an opportunity to constrain orbital parameters needed for life.

Enceladus · Saturn system
The central idea

Connect what we observe today to how the system evolved and how that can be used to constrain parameters which planets/moons need to host life.

Cassini observations constrain the present-day Saturnian system. Numerical simulations can then test which possible earlier orbital configurations could evolve toward the system we observe

01 / Observe

Cassini data

Use spacecraft measurements and published planetary parameters to establish constraints on Enceladus and its neighboring moons.

02 / Simulate

Orbital evolution

Explore candidate initial configurations with N-body simulations and investigate stability and orbital relationships.

03 / Interpret

Habitability context

Connect ocean worlds, tidal heating, liquid water, and planetary evolution to the broader search for extraterrestrial life.

About me

Curiosity about the universe

Hello! my name is Ishan Prabhu and I am an IT major at Innovation Academy. I love almost everything astronomy related, but I specifically am interested in astrophysics and the propect of extraterrestrial life. When I grow up, I want to become an astrophysicist

This project combines my interest in astronomy and astrophysics with my interest in extraterrestrial life. By constraining initial orbital parameters of moons, I can help astronomers in the future find out which planets can host life and which ones cannot by eliminating a large subset of options or providing a small subset of options for them to check.
